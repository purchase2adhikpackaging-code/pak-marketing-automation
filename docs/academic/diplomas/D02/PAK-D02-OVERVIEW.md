## Programme Purpose

PAK-D02 develops railway-focused mechanical technicians and junior engineering professionals with a broader mechanical engineering base than D01. Graduates should be able to interpret mechanical drawings, analyze forces and machine elements, understand materials and manufacturing processes, work with fluid and thermal systems, perform measurements and inspections, support railway mechanical maintenance, diagnose failures, and participate in workshop overhaul and engineering improvement activities.

The curriculum progresses from engineering mechanics, mathematics, materials, CAD and workshop foundations in Semester 1; through strength of materials, machine systems, thermofluids, manufacturing and railway mechanical applications in Semester 2; into machine design, maintenance, tribology, inspection, failure analysis and workshop integration in Semester 3; and finally into advanced overhaul, reliability, digital manufacturing, maintenance planning and industry application in Semester 4.

## Curriculum Design Principles

1. Mechanical depth with railway context.
2. Shared-core efficiency only where outcomes truly match.
3. Design-to-maintenance continuity.
4. Measurement before judgement.
5. Progressive workshop competence.
6. Failure-oriented engineering.
7. Modern mechanical practice through CAD/CAM, reverse engineering, condition monitoring and reliability.

## Shared-Module Strategy

Potential shared families with D01 include railway systems fundamentals, engineering drawing, materials/corrosion, workshop safety/metrology, welding/fabrication fundamentals, NDT/inspection fundamentals, maintenance engineering and quality/traceability. D02 retains deeper mechanical treatment in mechanics, strength of materials, machine elements, thermofluids, machine design, tribology, vibration, manufacturing and CAD/CAM.

## Technical & Regulatory Baseline

Use current authoritative EU/Polish railway requirements where mechanical work interfaces with regulated systems, applicable TSIs, ERA guidance, EN/ISO engineering standards, recognized mechanical-engineering sources and approved manufacturer maintenance instructions where legitimate.

**Control rule:** generic academic formulas or workshop practices must not be presented as railway acceptance criteria unless an applicable approved source establishes that criterion.
