## Programme Purpose

PAK-D04 prepares operations coordinators, transport-planning assistants, terminal/yard personnel, junior traffic-management professionals, freight/passenger operations staff and future railway managers with a structured understanding of how railway services are planned, controlled, measured and improved.

The programme combines railway-system knowledge with transport economics, operations rules, timetable/capacity concepts, passenger and freight service planning, yards/terminals, traffic control, disruption management, safety management, supply-chain coordination, customer experience, commercial practice, sustainability and operational analytics.

The programme is educational and managerial in scope. It does **not** by itself confer competence, licensing or employer authorization for safety-critical functions such as train driving, signaller/dispatcher duties, traffic-control authority, shunting command, safety-critical communications or infrastructure access where authorization is required.

## Shared-Module Strategy

Technical diploma content may be reused only at the awareness depth required by operations students. D04 requires independent treatment for train operations/traffic management, timetable/capacity, passenger service, freight/intermodal logistics, stations/yards/terminals, control-centre decisions, disruption, transport economics/commercial management, passenger demand/revenue/customer experience, cross-border operations and operations analytics.

## Regulatory & Safety Baseline

Future chapters must verify current EU interoperability/safety requirements, current OPE TSI, applicable telematics rules and ERA guidance, infrastructure-manager/railway-undertaking rules where legitimate, Polish requirements and relevant passenger-rights, dangerous-goods, working-time, procurement, accessibility and data-protection rules as applicable.

Operational examples must never be generalized into real movement authorities, degraded-mode instructions or dispatching rules without named governing sources. Safety-critical scenarios remain simulation-only unless delivered within an authorized competence framework.
