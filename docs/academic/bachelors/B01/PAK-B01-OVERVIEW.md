## Programme Purpose

PAK-B01 is the broad multidisciplinary engineering spine of the PAK Bachelor's-level portfolio. It develops integrated engineering understanding across railway infrastructure, rolling stock, traction and energy, signalling and control, operations, safety/RAMS, digital systems, asset management, project/lifecycle engineering and professional practice.

The programme is deliberately positioned above the applied Diploma layer. Diploma programmes emphasize technician and junior-engineering competence, maintenance practice, inspection, workshop activity, controlled diagnostics and applied operations. B01 adds higher engineering science, quantitative analysis, modelling and simulation, design reasoning, subsystem/interface engineering, RAMS and lifecycle trade-offs, optimization, research methods, multidisciplinary design projects and a final capstone/thesis.

B01 is not intended to replace the specialist Bachelor's programmes. It provides the common systems-oriented engineering spine while B02–B05 retain deeper specialization value.

## Curriculum Design Principles

1. **Engineering science before specialization** — mathematics, physics, mechanics, materials, electrical science and computation precede advanced railway subsystem work.
2. **Systems integration over siloed subsystems** — students must understand interactions between infrastructure, vehicles, energy, signalling, operations and organizational systems.
3. **Analysis and design above Diploma depth** — subjects must add quantitative analysis, modelling, design or verification reasoning beyond applied technician-level treatment.
4. **Safety/RAMS by design** — safety, reliability, availability, maintainability, human factors and evidence-based assurance are built into the progression.
5. **Lifecycle engineering and asset thinking** — design, operation, maintenance, renewal, economics and sustainability are treated as connected lifecycle decisions.
6. **Digital and computational readiness** — programming, simulation, data analytics, IoT, predictive maintenance and cybersecurity awareness support modern railway engineering.
7. **Practical engineering supports analytical reasoning** — laboratories, workshops, field exercises and simulations reinforce engineering judgement rather than replace it with craft-only training.
8. **Regulatory traceability without false authorization claims** — EU/Polish railway requirements and technical standards are referenced without implying professional or statutory authority.
9. **Research, professional practice and capstone integration** — the final year requires research discipline, supervised industry exposure and defensible multidisciplinary engineering work.

## Academic Depth Rule

B01 subjects must not simply re-code Diploma content. Each subject must add one or more of the following where relevant: mathematical derivation, quantitative analysis, engineering design/sizing reasoning, simulation/modelling, subsystem interface analysis, lifecycle/reliability trade-offs, verification/validation reasoning, optimization, research-based analysis or multidisciplinary systems integration.

## Safety & Authorization Boundary

PAK-B01 is academic engineering education. Completion **does not authorize** train driving, dispatching, signalling operation, shunting command, safety-critical signalling design approval, testing or commissioning sign-off, traction-power switching, high-voltage operational authority, independent maintenance release/return-to-service, independent NDT certification/sign-off, formal ECM or conformity-assessment authority, statutory infrastructure inspection/sign-off, or any employer/jurisdiction-specific licensed or authorized railway duty.

Safety-critical parameters, limits, procedures, test settings and acceptance/rejection criteria must come from approved current controlled sources and competent authorized organizations/personnel.

## Regulatory & Technical Reference Baseline

Future chapter development must verify current official editions and applicability of:

- Directive (EU) 2016/797 — railway interoperability;
- Directive (EU) 2016/798 — railway safety;
- applicable TSIs including INF, ENE, LOC&PAS, WAG, CCS, OPE and relevant telematics instruments;
- Regulation (EU) 2019/779 — ECM;
- ERA Common Safety Methods, application guides and technical guidance;
- applicable EN/IEC/ISO railway, RAMS, electrical, structural, signalling, cybersecurity, quality, NDT and asset-management standards;
- relevant Polish railway law, national rules and competent-authority requirements;
- approved infrastructure-manager, railway-undertaking, manufacturer and maintenance-organization controlled documents where legitimately available.

Regulatory references are curriculum anchors, not substitutes for legal review. Proprietary or copyrighted standards text must not be reproduced beyond permitted use. Publication-time revalidation is mandatory.
