## Professional Practice & Capstone Controls

Semester 6 integrates advanced vehicle-performance modelling with fleet/depot engineering, reliability analytics, research and supervised professional practice. B02-601 must demonstrate engineering understanding of wheel–rail contact, running performance and dynamic behaviour without implying test/acceptance authority. B02-602/B02-603 connect maintenance planning, fleet availability, condition data and reliability evidence to defensible lifecycle decisions.

Internship activity must be governed by the host site's competence, supervision, access and authorization rules. Student status does **not authorize** independent safety-critical vehicle operation, inspection, brake release, high-voltage work, NDT sign-off, maintenance release, software changes or certification/homologation decisions.

The final capstone/thesis must define a defensible rolling-stock engineering problem, assumptions and data provenance, quantitative analysis or modelling, subsystem/interface and safety considerations, technical/regulatory reference traceability, verification reasoning, limitations and clear professional technical communication. It should demonstrate Bachelor's-level vehicle-engineering reasoning rather than merely document workshop activity.
